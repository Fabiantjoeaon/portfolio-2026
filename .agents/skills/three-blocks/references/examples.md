- [Ocean · Raymarched Volume Water](https://threejs-blocks.com/examples/webgpu_simulation_water_ocean) — A 524,288-particle MLS-MPM/APIC ocean spans a 44×22 m surface and transfers mass and momentum through a 128×64×64 grid. - [Water Ball · Storm Sphere on a Checker](https://threejs-blocks.com/examples/webgpu_simulation_water_surface) — A 524,288-particle MLS-MPM/APIC WaterVolume held in a sphere with no container, after WaterBall: the domain box is inert and the fluid is shaped by a single gridForce hook, where gravity points at the ball's centre instead of down. A vector noise field drives storm cells blowing along the surface in every direction plus a radial swell pump, so waves keep breaking across the whole globe. Nearest-surface depth comes from ComputeSphereRasterizer used as a depth producer rather than a colour pass, replacing the usual per-particle hardware splat with one tiled binning and raster compute batch; optical thickness is the analytic chord through the ball, so no per-particle render pass runs at all. WaterSurfaceRenderer applies a world-space-sigma narrow-range filter (Truong & Yuksel, I3D 2018) at full resolution, and createWaterComposite shades the sheet with physical refraction over the four-quadrant calibration floor, analytic sky reflection, Beer-Lambert absorption, glitter, whitewater, and a sun shadow ray-marched through the solver's density grid — cast onto the floor by intersecting the ball analytically and marching only the chord. Renderer: webgpu; techniques: MPM. Blocks: water, sphere-impostors.
